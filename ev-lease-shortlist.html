The take

What reviewers and owners consistently flag, condensed to what actually differs between these seven.

Hyundai Ioniq 5

from $439/mo (AWD)

A compact crossover, not a 3-row large SUV either — but it charges faster than anything else on this list (under 20 minutes 10–80%) and ties the R2 for the cheapest AWD entry point. The XRT trim adds genuine off-road hardware at the cost of range.

Pros

  • Fastest 10–80% DC charging of every vehicle on this list
  • Cheapest AWD entry point alongside the R2
  • XRT adds a real lift, all-terrain tires, and terrain drive modes

Cons

  • Only 5 seats, no third row — the smallest, most compact vehicle here
  • XRT's off-road gear cuts range to 259 mi and roughens the ride
  • Only the XRT is tow-rated, and only to 2,700 lb with a factory hitch

Rivian R2

from $829/mo

Rivian's smaller, 5-seat sibling to the R1S — not actually large or three-row like the rest of this list, but it undercuts every other AWD long-range EV here on price. Only the top Performance trim is shipping today.

Pros

  • Cheapest AWD long-range EV on this list by a wide margin
  • 656-hp Performance AWD: 3.6s 0–60 with 29-min 10–80% charging
  • R1S-level build/off-road reputation in a smaller footprint

Cons

  • Only 5 seats, no third row — a genuinely smaller vehicle than the rest of this list
  • Current lease ($829/mo) runs close to the much larger R1S's, blunting the value case
  • Cheaper Premium AWD and RWD trims don't ship until late 2026/2027 — today it's Performance or nothing

Kia EV9

from $409/mo

The value play — genuine 3-row room and Kia's fastest charging for roughly two-thirds the price of the rest of this list.

Pros

  • Spacious, well-finished 3-row cabin
  • 24-min fast charging matches pricier rivals
  • Quiet at speed; strong feature-for-dollar value

Cons

  • Big footprint, unwieldy in tight SoCal parking
  • Driver-assist alerts are persistent and intrusive
  • Cabin materials feel plain next to luxury rivals

Hyundai Ioniq 9

from ~$419/mo

The roomiest cabin and cargo hold here, with genuinely quick charging — at a real premium over Hyundai's own gas Palisade.

Pros

  • Adult-usable third row and best cargo-behind-3rd-row figure
  • 350 kW charging, one of the fastest here
  • Solid, well-equipped interior build

Cons

  • ~$20k more than a comparable Palisade
  • CarPlay/SiriusXM and split-screen infotainment quirks
  • Lane-keep assist reads as overly intrusive

VW ID. Buzz

from ~$838/mo (2025MY)

The most distinctive thing on this list and the cleverest packaging — up to 145.5 ft³ with the rows pulled out — but the shortest range, slowest charging, and lightest tow rating here, and the US is skipping the 2026 model year entirely.

Pros

  • Standout retro-modern design and genuinely flexible seating/cargo
  • Removable third row unlocks van-like max cargo room
  • 4MOTION AWD adds real traction and 335 hp for ~$4,500

Cons

  • Shortest range (231–234 mi) and slowest charging (200 kW) here
  • Lowest tow rating (2,700 lb) — and RWD isn't tow-rated at all
  • Skips MY2026 in the US; current stock is leftover 2025MY ahead of a 2027 redesign

Volvo EX90

~$800–900/mo (broker est.)

The calmest, most Volvo-like experience of the group — refined and safety-forward, but the shortest cargo hold and lowest tow rating here.

Pros

  • Smooth, quiet cruiser with premium materials
  • Class-leading driver-assist and safety sensor suite
  • 2026's 800V update brings genuinely fast charging

Cons

  • Just 11 ft³ behind the third row — tight for luggage
  • Heavy reliance on touchscreen for basics like mirrors
  • Lowest max tow rating (4,850 lb) of the seven

Rivian R1S

$779/mo

The most capable and quickest-feeling of the group on- and off-road, with a tow rating built for real trailers.

Pros

  • Genuine off-road ability plus refined daily manners
  • 7,700 lb tow rating, best outside the Escalade IQ
  • Strong build quality and over-the-air support

Cons

  • Nearly everything routes through the touchscreen
  • Slowest 10–80% charge time on this list
  • Base trim's 270-mi range is the shortest here

Lucid Gravity

~$1,099/mo (Grand Touring)

The range and efficiency leader by a wide margin, and reviewers say it drives smaller than its size — but Lucid's software polish still lags.

Pros

  • 450-mi Grand Touring range, longest of the group
  • Handles like a sedan despite 3-row SUV size
  • Spacious, genuinely usable third row for adults

Cons

  • Owners report app freezes and navigation glitches
  • Driver-assist trails Tesla/GM's systems
  • Least-proven brand; some features ship "later"

Tesla Model X

~$999/mo (est.)

The quickest and best-networked for road trips via Supercharger — but the shortest range and tightest third row on this list.

Pros

  • Largest, most reliable DC fast-charging network
  • Strong performance; flexible 5/6/7-seat configs
  • Frequent OTA feature and software updates

Cons

  • Shortest EPA range of the seven
  • Third row is tight, best for kids on longer trips
  • Long-running build-quality and panel-gap complaints

Cadillac Escalade IQ

$2,464/mo

The biggest, longest-range, and most tow-capable vehicle here — priced and sized like the flagship it is.

Pros

  • ~460-mi estimated range with an 8,000 lb tow rating
  • Full-size 3-row comfort; Super Cruise hands-free driving
  • 55-inch curved display and genuinely premium features

Cons

  • 9,100+ lb curb weight blunts handling despite 750 hp
  • Most controls buried in the touchscreen, not buttons
  • Public DC fast-charging sessions run into triple digits

Before you sign, in California

  • No state EV rebate applies here. CVRP closed to new applicants in November 2023; the remaining state programs (Clean Cars 4 All, DCAP) are income-qualified and unlikely to apply to a lease in this price range.
  • The federal $7,500 credit is gone — for both purchases and leases — as of September 30, 2025. Any "$7,500 credit" in a current lease offer (Rivian, Cadillac) is manufacturer-funded incentive cash, not a tax credit.
  • No more solo carpool-lane access. California's Clean Air Vehicle HOV decal program expired October 1, 2025, so a new EV lease no longer comes with single-occupant carpool-lane eligibility.
  • Lease figures above are snapshots, not quotes. Money factor, residual value, and manufacturer lease cash shift monthly and by credit tier — treat every number here as a starting point to confirm with SoCal dealers, not a locked rate.
  • Charge times assume a compatible high-power DC fast charger at or near ideal battery temperature; real-world sessions (Electrify America, EVgo) commonly run longer, especially in summer heat.
  • The ID. Buzz you'd lease today is 2025 model-year stock. Volkswagen is skipping the US 2026 model year and holding the next update for 2027 — expect leftover-inventory dealer discounts, but also thinner color/trim selection.
